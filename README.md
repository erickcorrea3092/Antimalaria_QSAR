# Antimalaria_QSAR

Artemisinin and its derivatives are the most potent antimalarials available, with the lowest propensity for the development of resistance; however, their administration is limited by issues regarding bioavailability. Consequently, various derivatives have been synthesized from artemisinin and dihydroartemisinin. It is essential to develop new artemisinin derivatives with improved pharmacological properties, such as enhanced bioavailability and absorption; this would prolong their therapeutic effect, reduce the required dosage, and decrease the likelihood of resistance developing. We believe that structural modifications to the artemisinin molecule will enhance its antimalarial activity. In this work, we focus on the design and synthesis of artemisinin derivatives with increased antimalarial activity, employing both *in silico* development and experimental synthesis of new derivatives with potential as antimalarials. Artemisitone-9 will be used as a starting material across three synthetic routes. We have currently gathered data to construct a QSAR (Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship) model based on 63 molecules. Currently our model presents this metrics: r² value of 0.64, a q² CV-LMO of 0.12, and a q²ext of 0.24, and our group is working in enhance the model.
